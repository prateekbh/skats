# SWAT Kats: Megakat Skies

An unofficial, open-source fan game built in **Godot 4.7**. Climb into the TurboKat with
Razor and T-Bone and defend Megakat City from Dark Kat, one episode at a time:
arcade jet combat, lock-on missiles and big set-piece bosses, playable in a web browser.

## Play

| Platform | How |
|---|---|
| Browser | Every push to `main` builds and publishes to GitHub Pages (see `.github/workflows/web.yml`) |
| Desktop | Open the folder in Godot 4.7 and press **F5** |

### Controls

| Action | Keyboard / Mouse | Gamepad |
|---|---|---|
| Pitch / Roll | Arrow keys | Left stick |
| Rudder | A / D | Right stick |
| Throttle | W / S | RT / LT |
| Afterburner | Shift | RB |
| Cannon | Space / Left click | A |
| Blowtorch homing missile (lock first) | F / Right click | B |
| Fire selected special missile | R | X |
| Next special missile | Q | LB |
| Airbrake (tighter turns) | Hold S at idle | Hold LT at idle |
| Camera (chase / far / cockpit) | C | Y |
| Invert pitch · Mute · Pause | Y · M · Esc | Start |

Missiles lock on by themselves: keep a target in front of you until the diamond turns red.

**Touch (phones and tablets, landscape only):** left thumb: a throttle slider with the BOOST flame on top (hold), a gun icon (hold to fire),
MISSILE (homing) and a round special-missile carousel (tap to fire, swipe to change). Right thumb: drag anywhere on the right half
to steer (pitch and roll; banking turns the nose). Held upright, the page shows a "rotate your device" screen
and the game pauses. Touch devices go fullscreen when a stage is launched.

## What's in v0.1

- **The TurboKat:** a community 3D model painted in the show's colours (glossy black, red cockpit pod with royal-blue glass, blue wingtips and nose, red engines with spinning turbines). Its wings swing back at high speed and spread open when slow, and it's cel-shaded with cartoon outlines. A procedural fallback airframe is built if the model files are missing.
- **Launcher:** a comic-book page. Razor and T-Bone, Callie, Dark Kat and Turmoil in ink-bordered panels around a big centre panel with our TurboKat over Megakat City and the menu (stages, briefings, controls, settings). The panels slam in one by one on open while "Big Trouble" plays, and the game loads behind the page. Choosing a stage plays the episode's opening story as a comic strip with its own music (Esc skips), then the launch: the salvage-yard hatch opens and the TurboKat bursts out and climbs away over the city.
- **Arcade flight model:** bank-to-turn, auto-level, afterburner, speed gained in dives and lost in climbs.
- **Weapons:** twin cannons with overheat, Blowtorch homing missiles, and a rack of the show's
  special missiles (from the swatkats.info weapons catalogue): the **Octopus** (captures a target),
  **Buzz-Saw** (saw barrage, one per locked target), **Scrambler** (shorts out everything nearby),
  **Slicer** (cuts through armor) and **Match Head** (incendiary blast). Each one flies its own
  way with its own trail (Octopus swims on a pink ink wave, Buzz-Saw corkscrews in sparks,
  Scrambler zigzags like lightning, Slicer streaks like a laser, Match Head lobs a fiery arc),
  and firing one plays a short launch shot: a comic title card and a slow-motion beat beside the
  jet as the missile leaves the rack. Skip it with any fire button, or turn it off in the pause
  menu. The selected missile shows as a card with its picture from the show (from the
  swatkats.info missile catalogue) on a cockpit weapons screen; cycling with Q slides the cards
  across it like a carousel. The launch intro plays over the theme music only.
- **Cutting kills and city damage:** a Slicer kill slices a Creepling into five pieces and a
  Buzz-Saw kill saws it in two, with no explosion: the pieces tumble away with glowing cut
  edges, electric sparks and smoke. Missiles that hit buildings bend them out of shape (a crater
  punched in at the hit, the tower leaning away, a jagged broken roofline), knock roofs down
  with rubble, topple tower tiers and leave burning holes, and leave thick black smoke columns that
  keep rising until the stage ends, so the damage to Megakat City stays visible.
- **Cartoon effects:** banded toon fireballs that break up into chunky smoke, impact stars,
  shockwave rings, debris streaks, puffy missile trails, speed lines on afterburner and hit-stop on
  big kills. All pooled (no per-explosion allocations) to keep the web build smooth.
- **Megakat City:** a 15 x 10 km world from a small data plan (`data/city_plan.json`): a Manhattan-dense downtown of towers up to 520 m around the 1,200 m MegaKat Tower, City Hall with a working clock tower, Enforcer HQ, commercial towers, residential neighbourhoods, an industrial waterfront with piers, cranes and ships, a park with a lake, the salvage yard with Jake & Chance's Garage, and mountains, a river, a dam, beaches and the open sea around it. Streets are tight canyons (32-85 m, wider avenues) but always connected, and three high bridges can be flown under. Supply caches with light beams sit near the ground (fly low to grab them; in Free Flight some are guarded by flak nests, Episode 1 is planes only) and are where you restock special missiles. Pitch is inverted by default (toggle with Y).
- **Episode 1, *Dark Skies Over Megakat*:** two Creepling waves, then Dark Kat's own jet: it flies inside an energy shield and fires lasers; two missile hits show the shield works, then a cutscene arms two Scrambler Missiles. Miss both and it's game over, hit once and the shield drops. Original radio dialogue for Razor, T-Bone, Callie, Feral and Dark Kat.
- **Free Flight:** endless escalating waves for score attack.
- **Story:** radio chatter arrives as comic cards with the speaker's portrait and an expression for
  the line, and between the parts of an episode the game pauses for comic-strip story beats from
  Commander Feral, Lt. Felina Feral, Mayor Manx, Deputy Mayor Callie Briggs, Ann Gora and Dark Kat.
  Episode 1 is in three parts with an opening story and an epilogue. After the hatch launch
  the player has the controls straight away.
- **Presentation:** HUD with radar, lock-on and warnings; briefing and debrief screens with ranks and saved best scores; all sound effects synthesized at runtime.

See [`docs/GDD.md`](docs/GDD.md) for the design document, Season 1 episode plan and roadmap.

## Project layout

```
scenes/main.tscn              entry scene → scripts/main.gd (state machine)
scripts/autoload/             Game (input, settings, saves, episodes) and Sfx (synthesized audio)
scripts/player/               TurboKat flight model, procedural model, chase camera
scripts/combat/               Target base class; bullets, missiles, explosions, pickups
scripts/enemies/              enemies shared by episodes: Creepling gliders/bombers, ground flak
scripts/missions/mission.gd   the Mission base class every episode extends
episodes/<name>/              one self-contained folder per episode (mission, boss, shaders, card)
scripts/world/city.gd         Megakat City generator + collision height field
scripts/ui/                   HUD and menus (built in code)
shaders/                      buildings, ground, water
```

## Automated testing

`main.gd` includes an autopilot, so CI and contributors can smoke-test without playing:

```bash
godot --headless --path . --import
```

```bash
godot --headless --path . -- --autotest --seconds=60
```

```bash
godot --headless --path . -- --autotest --god --seconds=600
```

```bash
xvfb-run godot --path . --rendering-driver opengl3 -- --showcase=/tmp/shots
```

`--god` makes the player invulnerable with perfect aim, which checks mission logic rather than
flying skill. `--showcase` saves screenshots of the TurboKat from six angles.

## Adding an episode

Each episode is one folder under `episodes/` with an `episode.gd` (menu card) and a `mission.gd`
(extends `Mission`), plus any boss, shader or art only that episode uses. Register the folder in
`Game.EPISODE_FOLDERS`. Full steps in [`episodes/README.md`](episodes/README.md).

## Analytics and privacy

The hosted web build sends anonymous gameplay events (stage picked, progress, win or lose, which
missiles are used) to Google Analytics so we can improve the game. It is skipped when your browser
sends Do Not Track, and nothing is sent from desktop builds or tests. Details and the full event
list: [`docs/ANALYTICS.md`](docs/ANALYTICS.md).
